Bij AI

Variant prioritisation for clinicians and pathologists.

Bij AI reads a VCF and returns a draft ACMG/AMP classification for every variant, with the evidence and the reasoning that produced it — for a qualified clinician or pathologist to review and sign off.

Bij AI produces draft classifications requiring qualified human review. It does not provide final clinical interpretation.

Where Bij AI stands today

Bij AI is a variant prioritisation system in active development. It has not been clinically validated, holds no regulatory approval or certification, and is not a medical device. It is not a substitute for professional clinical genetic interpretation, diagnosis, or advice.

From VCF to a reviewable draft

Bij AI takes variant calls and assembles, for each variant, the evidence a reviewer would otherwise gather by hand: clinical database records, population frequencies, protein and structural annotation, and computational predictions. It then evaluates the ACMG/AMP criteria against that evidence and shows its working.

  • Evidence assembly

    Public and curated sources are queried per variant and returned together, each labelled with where it came from.

  • ACMG/AMP criteria evaluation

    Bij AI evaluates 19 of the 28 ACMG/AMP criteria, each reported as triggered, not triggered, or not evaluated, with its rationale. The remaining nine have no integrated data source and are always reported as not evaluated rather than silently omitted.

  • Transparent reasoning

    Every criterion carries its decision path, its supporting and conflicting evidence, and the sources it relied on.

  • Prioritisation

    Variants are scored for confidence and review priority so a reviewer can start where it matters.

How it works

  1. VCF
  2. Annotation, models, databases, interpretation
  3. Draft clinical report

Upstream processing from FASTQ — quality control, alignment and variant calling — is also available as a separate stage for labs that need it.

A draft report is not clinically actionable. Mandatory qualified human review and final sign-off stand between Bij AI’s output and any clinical use.

Reasoning you can audit

Bij AI is built so a reviewer can disagree with it. Each criterion states the path it took to its conclusion, and records what it could not check as explicitly as what it could — unverified caveats are printed, not omitted.

Where a source returned nothing, the report distinguishes not queried from queried, nothing found from lookup failed. An absence is never presented as a finding.
