About Bij AI

Bij AI is a variant prioritisation system built for the part of clinical genetics that does not scale: reading the evidence for one variant at a time.

A qualified reviewer classifying a variant gathers records from clinical databases, checks population frequencies, looks at protein domains and structure, weighs computational predictions, and applies the ACMG/AMP criteria to what they find. Bij AI does that assembly and produces a draft, with its reasoning attached, so the reviewer’s time goes to judgement rather than collection.

A draft, not a decision

Bij AI produces a draft classification requiring qualified human review and final sign-off. It does not independently provide final clinical interpretation. This is a design decision, not a disclaimer bolted on afterwards: the system records what it could not determine as carefully as what it could, and it declines to state a conclusion the evidence does not carry.

Every report Bij AI generates carries this position in its own text. The reviewer, not the system, is the one who classifies.

What is in the pipeline

Evidence sources

Bij AI queries clinical variant databases, population frequency resources, gene–disease and dosage curations, protein sequence and domain annotation, predicted protein structure, and phenotype and rare-disease resources. Each result is recorded with its provenance.

Computational models

Bij AI integrates existing pretrained genomic and protein models as published by their authors. No model is retrained or fine-tuned. Models are one input among several, and which of them apply depends on the variant and on how the system is configured for a given run — a run’s report states which models were used and which were not.

ACMG/AMP evaluation

Bij AI evaluates 19 of the 28 ACMG/AMP criteria individually against the assembled evidence. The remaining nine have no integrated data source and are always reported as not evaluated rather than silently omitted.

Triggered criteria are combined using the Tavtigian et al. 2018 Bayesian-calibrated point system, producing a candidate classification for a qualified human to confirm.

Saying “we do not know” is a feature

A system that fills gaps with plausible values is worse than one that leaves them open, because a plausible gap is indistinguishable from a finding. Bij AI separates not queried from queried and genuinely absent from lookup failed, and reports unchecked caveats alongside the criteria they qualify.

Where a run is incomplete, the report says so rather than presenting partial results as whole.

Governance

Provenance and review

Each report records the sources consulted, the code version that produced it, and its review state. Reports are produced as drafts and remain drafts until a qualified reviewer signs them off.

Status

Bij AI is a variant prioritisation system in active development. It has not been clinically validated, holds no regulatory approval or certification, and is not a medical device. It produces draft classifications requiring qualified human review and final sign-off before any clinical use.
